## Direct answer

Evaluate an AI agent agency in Georgia (Tbilisi) the same way you would evaluate one anywhere: workflow mapping before build, human approval gates on risky actions, written acceptance tests, evals, and a real handoff package. The local layer adds four checks: time-zone overlap with your team, delivery language for each artifact (EN/RU/KA), contracting jurisdiction, and whether the price advantage survives production requirements. Georgia's UTC+4 clock and cost structure fit EU and Gulf buyers well; the production bar does not move because the office is in Tbilisi.

## Why Tbilisi shows up on shortlists

Georgia's tech sector has grown fast on a few structural advantages: company registration measured in days, IT-friendly tax regimes, an English-working engineering market, and an inflow of relocated senior engineers since 2022. For a buyer this translates into real senior capacity at rates below Western Europe and the US.

It does not translate into automatic quality. Demo theater exists at every price point. Use the address to explain the price; use process artifacts to predict the outcome.

## Time zones and collaboration

Georgia runs on UTC+4 all year, with no daylight saving shifts. That makes overlap planning unusually predictable.

| Your team | Offset from Tbilisi | Practical overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western Europe (CET) | 2-3 hours behind | Most of the workday |
| UK | 3-4 hours behind | Tbilisi afternoon = London morning to midday |
| Gulf (UAE, KSA) | 0-1 hours | Effectively same-day, full overlap |
| US East Coast | 8-9 hours behind | Tbilisi late afternoon = US morning |
| US West Coast | 11-12 hours behind | Async by default, one narrow window |

Ask any candidate agency to name, in writing, the hours they commit to synchronous work and who answers escalations outside them. "We are flexible" is not an answer; a named delivery lead with stated hours is.

## Rates and what they actually buy

Treat every number here as a typical market range, not a quote from Dali or any specific vendor. Blended agency rates for AI and automation work in Tbilisi commonly land in the $25-60 per hour band, against $100-250 for comparable seniority in the US and Western Europe. Fixed-scope agent pilots priced from local cost bases tend to come in well under Western equivalents for the same scope.

The trap is reading the delta as free money. A low rate on a team that skips evals, approval gates, and observability is not savings; rework and incidents eat the difference by month three. Score production practice first, then let price break ties between teams that pass.

## Jurisdiction, contracts, and payments

- **Entity.** Most Tbilisi teams operate a Georgian LLC; some keep an EU or US entity for procurement comfort. Either works if the contract is clean.
- **Tax regimes.** Georgia offers IT-oriented statuses (Virtual Zone, International Company) that keep local cost bases sustainable. Terms change; verify current rules with counsel.
- **Contract basics.** English-language contracts, explicit IP assignment, and an agreed governing law are standard practice. Ask for all three; hesitation on IP assignment is a disqualifier.
- **Payments.** Standard invoicing and SWIFT transfers work routinely; card rails vary by bank.
- **Data.** If you are under GDPR, confirm where logs, traces, and prompts are stored and sign a data processing agreement before any production data flows.

## Local, remote, or hybrid

Local presence helps exactly two things: discovery workshops and change management with your staff. Everything else in agent delivery is remote-friendly. The pattern that works for most buyers is hybrid: on-site discovery if you have people in Georgia, then remote build with weekly demos, approval gates, and written acceptance tests. Do not pay a premium for a local office if what you need is a gate map.

## Delivery language

Confirm three language layers separately, because they diverge in practice:

1. User-facing agent surfaces: Georgian only if your users need it.
2. Ops documentation, runbooks, and eval descriptions: usually English.
3. Day-to-day collaboration: English and Russian are both common in the Tbilisi market.

A team can demo in fluent English and still hand you runbooks your ops team cannot read. Put the language of each artifact into the contract.

## What to buy locally

A production pilot tied to your tools, not a generic chatbot package:

- One workflow, mapped in writing before any build starts
- Acceptance tests agreed up front, tied to finished work quality
- Approval gates on irreversible actions from day one, loosening as trust builds
- An eval set with regression checks before each release
- Logs and traces you can read without the vendor present
- A handoff package: docs, runbook, credentials plan, and defined exit terms

## Red flags, local edition

- A rate-first pitch with no discovery questions
- "We will add approvals later"
- A portfolio of demos with no production artifacts behind them
- No named delivery lead available in your working hours
- Vague answers on where your data and traces physically live

## FAQ

### Is local registration required to hire?
No. This is a procurement preference, not a technical requirement. Plenty of buyers contract a Georgian LLC directly under an agreed governing law; others route through a vendor's EU or US entity. Technical quality is independent of the paperwork path, so settle the entity question with procurement and spend your diligence time on process artifacts.

### Should we require Georgian-language UI agents?
Only if your users need Georgian. Most cross-border engagements ship English or Russian user surfaces. What matters either way is the ops layer: runbooks, escalation docs, and eval descriptions must be in a language your team actually reads, and that belongs in the contract, not in assumptions.

### What do Tbilisi agencies typically charge for agent work?
As typical market ranges only: blended rates commonly sit in the $25-60 per hour band, with fixed-scope pilots priced accordingly. Compare total first-year cost - build, retainer, LLM usage, your team's time - rather than the hourly sticker, and treat any vendor who cannot estimate LLM costs at your volume as unproven.

### How much time-zone overlap should we require?
Four hours of daily overlap is a comfortable floor for an active build; two hours works once the system is in steady state with a good escalation path. Georgia's fixed UTC+4 makes this easy for Europe and the Gulf and workable for the US East Coast.

### Can a Georgian vendor handle GDPR workloads?
Yes, if the engineering is done properly: EU-hosted data and traces, a signed DPA, scoped credentials, and documented retention. Ask where every copy of your data lives, including prompts and logs. A vendor who has done this before answers in specifics.

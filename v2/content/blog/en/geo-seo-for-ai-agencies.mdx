## Direct answer

For an AI agency, **SEO and GEO are the same job with two surfaces**: classic search results and AI answers that cite or summarize the web. You still need crawlable pages, clear entities, honest expertise, and content that answers real buyer questions. There is no separate "ChatGPT ranking trick" that replaces that work.

## What buyers actually search

- Agency vs freelancers vs in-house
- How to put agents into existing tools
- Approval gates and risk
- What "production agents" means in practice
- Local/regional partners (language, time zone, market)

Your content system should answer those questions with pages that can be indexed and quoted.

## System layers

1. **Technical eligibility** - indexable routes, sitemap, canonicals, hreflang for locales
2. **Entity clarity** - who Dali is, what you ship, founders, projects, contact
3. **Decision content** - comparisons and tutorials with failure modes
4. **Citability** - short direct-answer blocks under question headings
5. **Distribution** - profiles and directories only when truthful and maintained

## Multi-language note (EN / RU / GE / ARM)

Ship factual parity across locales. Local examples can differ; claims cannot contradict. Shared slug families and hreflang keep the cluster clean.

## What not to do

- Thin pages for every keyword variation
- Invented case metrics to look authoritative
- Treating llms.txt as a magic ranking lever
- Publishing EN only when you promise four languages

## FAQ

### Is GEO different from SEO?
Google frames AI features as grounded in the same eligibility systems as Search. Optimize for useful, indexable pages first.

### Do we need a blog if we have service pages?
Service pages rarely answer comparison and how-to questions in depth. A blog cluster fills that gap.

### How fast should we publish?
Cadence beats bursts. A steady stream of grounded posts compounds better than twenty thin pages in a week.

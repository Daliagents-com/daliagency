## Direct answer

There is no universal 'best AI agent agency.' Rank by fit for your workflow risk, not by logo walls. Prefer studios that map process, place human approval gates, ship inside tools you already use, and leave ownership with your team.

## Why 'best of' lists mislead

Most roundups reward paid placement, content volume, or brand size. They rarely test production failure modes: secrets, outbound messages, CRM writes, or stop-switches. Use lists only as a discovery set, then evaluate each firm against the same pilot criteria.

## Evaluation criteria that matter

Process discovery before build. Explicit patch vs rewrite thinking for existing systems. Human gates on irreversible actions. Monitoring and handoff docs. Fixed pilot boundary with an acceptance test. Real project write-ups without invented metrics.

## Signals of a production-ready partner

They ask about exceptions before demos. They refuse to auto-send sensitive messages on day one. They name tools and ownership. They show how work stops when confidence is low.

## Red flags

Only chat widgets as 'agents.' No approval model. Guaranteed ROI numbers with no measurement plan. Pressure to buy a platform before mapping one workflow.

## FAQ

### Can a tiny studio beat a large agency?
Yes, if they own the workflow and gates better. Size without ops discipline still ships demos.

### Should we require case studies with revenue metrics?
Prefer process proof and architecture over unverifiable revenue claims.

### How many agencies should we shortlist?
Two to four. More than that usually means the brief is unclear.

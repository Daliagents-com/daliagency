## Direct answer

Do not approve a production rollout just because the demos look polished.

Choose the partner that can show a production evidence gate for one real workflow.

That gate should prove the workflow boundary, the permission model, the logging path, the evaluation bar, the failure drill, the support owner, and the handoff package.

[IBM frames deployment](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agent-deployment) as moving from prototype or testing into real-world operation.

[Dataiku's production-ready guidance](https://www.dataiku.com/blog/how-to-build-production-ready-ai-agents) adds monitoring, access controls, rollback, and measurable success.

[OpenAI's practical guide](https://openai.com/business/guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-ai-agents/) is also useful because it keeps the discussion on workflows where agentic behavior actually makes sense.

If the workflow is still a rules engine problem, use software or internal automation first.

## The production evidence gate

Ask the partner to produce these artifacts for one workflow.

| Artifact | What it proves | Buyer verification | Production gate blocker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow boundary | The partner understands the exact path being automated. | Compare the document against your current SOP or ticket trail. | No single workflow, or scope still described as a platform. |
| Permission matrix | The partner knows which actions need least privilege or approval. | Check who can trigger reads, writes, sends, refunds, or deletes. | Shared admin access or no named gate owner. |
| Representative trace | The partner can show what a real run looks like. | Review one end-to-end sample with inputs, tool calls, and output. | No readable trace, or logs are not exportable. |
| Eval set and pass bar | The partner can measure quality instead of guessing. | Inspect the test cases, scoring notes, and threshold. | No named pass condition or no test set. |
| Rollback drill | The partner can stop or revert the workflow when it fails. | Ask them to demonstrate the failure path in writing. | No rollback path, or rollback depends on heroics. |
| Incident owner | Someone owns the next step when the system is wrong. | Confirm the named contact, response window, and escalation path. | Support is "best effort" only. |
| Handoff manifest | The partner has documented the artifacts, access, and operating knowledge required for handoff. | Review docs, runbook, prompt inventory, eval notes, and access list. | Handoff is described as informal or optional. |

Dali does not treat the engagement as production ready until the partner can produce all of those artifacts for the workflow under review.

## What to ask before production acceptance

- Show the workflow boundary as a written artifact.
- Show the permission matrix for every risky action.
- Show one representative trace from start to finish.
- Show the eval set and the pass bar.
- Show the rollback drill.
- Show the named incident owner and support window.
- Show the handoff manifest.

These are not sales questions.

They are production-acceptance questions.

## Why this gate exists

[NIST's AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) frames AI work as trust and risk management, not novelty.

[OWASP's 2026 agentic Top 10](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/) gives buyers current security language for autonomous agent systems.

[Gartner's warning about agent washing](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027) is useful even without the headline number because it reminds buyers that many products sound more agentic than they are.

That is why a partner checklist is not enough.

What matters is whether the partner can show evidence that a real workflow is controlled, inspectable, and supportable.

## Dali's recommendations

Dali recommends using one workflow per gate.

Dali recommends asking for the evidence pack before you accept a pilot as production ready.

Dali recommends blocking production acceptance or a phase-two commitment if the partner cannot show the trace, the pass bar, and the rollback path.

Dali does not recommend scoring the partner on generic "innovation" language.

## A compact decision rule

```text
boundary -> permissions -> trace -> eval -> rollback -> owner -> handoff
```

If one step is missing, the buyer does not have production evidence yet.

If the partner can only show a demo, the buyer still has a demo.

## FAQ

### Do we need ML PhDs on the vendor team?

The evidence gate does not require a specific degree.

It requires the team to demonstrate systems thinking, integrations, evaluation discipline, and operational ownership.

### Is multi-agent architecture required?

No.

[OpenAI recommends maximizing a single agent first](https://openai.com/business/guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-ai-agents/) before adding orchestration that may not be necessary.

### Can we test a partner quickly?

Yes.

Dali treats a paid audit as sufficient only when it produces the evidence artifacts needed for the next decision.

### Should the partner own the whole rollout?

Dali recommends that the partner own the part it can support cleanly.

Dali recommends that the buyer retain ownership of the business decision, approvals, and final operating model.

# X.com Article Draft

---

we analyzed 233 ai agencies to find out what they actually do, how they sell, and whether any of it is real.

most of it isn't.

we spent weeks building a competitive intelligence database. 233 companies. 43 data points per company. we scraped their websites, cross-referenced their claims on clutch and linkedin, verified their client lists, reverse-engineered their sales funnels, and tracked their founders' social media.

here is what we found. none of it is comfortable.

---

finding 1: the ai agency market is mostly rebranded dev shops

of the 233 companies we analyzed, at least 84 started as traditional software development shops or IT consultancies. they bolted "ai" onto their homepage sometime after 2023 and started selling "ai transformation" without meaningfully changing what they deliver.

the pattern is always the same. a company founded in 2008-2015. originally doing mobile apps, web development, or staff augmentation at $25-49/hr. then chatgpt happened. now their homepage says "ai-native development partner" but their clutch reviews are still about react dashboards and shopify integrations.

the rebrand is cosmetic. the delivery is the same. the pricing barely moved.

---

finding 2: 120 out of 233 companies rely on clutch as their primary sales channel

this was the most surprising number. over half the market is dependent on a single review platform for lead generation. 71 use linkedin as a major channel. 57 rely heavily on seo content.

almost nobody has a differentiated acquisition strategy. they all write the same blog posts about "how ai agents can transform your business." they all run the same clutch profile optimization playbook. they all post the same founder thought leadership on linkedin.

the result: a market where the loudest voice wins, not the best product.

---

finding 3: 88% of them sell "custom ai agents" but can't explain what that means

88 out of 233 companies list "agents" in their service offerings. 84 mention "automation." 61 offer "consulting." but when you dig into what they actually deliver, the specifics collapse.

the most common real stack across all 233 companies:

- n8n: 33 companies
- openai api: 30 companies
- make (formerly integromat): 23 companies
- langchain: 17 companies
- zapier: 13 companies

this means roughly a third of the entire ai agency market is building workflows in n8n and connecting them to the openai api. that is the "custom ai agent" they're selling for $25,000-$200,000.

there is nothing wrong with n8n or make. they are excellent tools. but selling an n8n workflow as a "custom ai agent" at enterprise pricing is the gap between marketing and reality.

---

finding 4: the pricing is a mess and nobody wants to be transparent

101 out of 233 companies have some form of public pricing data. the rest hide behind "contact us" and "book a demo."

here is the actual range we found:

- offshore dev shops (india, latam): $25-49/hr, projects from $2k-$150k
- mid-tier agencies (us/uk): $50-99/hr, projects from $25k-$200k
- enterprise consultancies: $150-300/hr, projects from $100k+
- boutique ai specialists: $5k-$50k fixed project, often with retainer upsell

the same deliverable — a chatbot connected to a knowledge base — can cost $3,000 from an indian dev shop or $150,000 from a us-based "ai consultancy." the chatbot is functionally identical.

---

finding 5: the sales funnel is copy-pasted across the entire market

37 companies offer a free consultation as the entry point. 47 offer an audit or readiness assessment. 57 have a retainer or ongoing support model as the upsell.

the playbook is universal:

free consultation → ai audit/readiness assessment → scoped project proposal → implementation → monthly retainer

every single agency runs some version of this funnel. the differentiation is zero. the language is interchangeable. you could swap the homepages of 50 of these companies and nobody would notice.

---

finding 6: most agencies have zero verifiable client results

this is the finding that should concern buyers most.

we specifically tracked case studies and named clients across all 233 companies. the pattern:

- 13 companies explicitly had no named clients at all
- dozens more list logos on their homepage but the work is unverifiable
- "case studies" are often hypothetical scenarios or demo deployments, not production implementations
- the most common proof format is clutch reviews, which are self-selected and unverifiable

when an agency says "we helped a fortune 500 company save $2m with ai automation" — in most cases, there is no named company, no named contact, no third-party verification.

---

finding 7: geography reveals the real business model

the geographic distribution tells you everything about how the market actually works:

- india: 48 companies
- us: 42 companies
- uk: 38 companies

many "us-based" or "uk-based" agencies are actually sales offices with indian or eastern european development teams. the business model is labor arbitrage disguised as ai expertise.

this is not inherently bad. but it matters when an agency charges $150/hr for "ai engineering" and the actual work is being done by a $25/hr developer in a different timezone.

---

finding 8: the market is selling fear, not outcomes

we tracked the marketing language of every company. the dominant angles:

- enterprise authority: 52 companies lead with enterprise credentials and trust signals
- social proof: 29 companies lead with logos, reviews, and partnerships
- trust and safety: 27 companies lead with "we're safe, compliant, reliable"
- risk reduction: 17 companies lead with "reduce risk of ai adoption"

only 11 companies lead with measurable roi. only 7 lead with speed.

the market sells ai the way consultancies have always sold: make the buyer feel safe about spending money, not confident about getting results.

---

finding 9: 18 companies were founded in 2023 or later. none have meaningful traction.

the post-chatgpt wave created 18 new agencies in our dataset. most are founder-led, 1-5 person shops. most have no named clients. most rely on the founder's linkedin presence for lead generation.

the pattern: founder posts about ai on linkedin → gets inbound from confused smbs → sells a $5k-$15k "ai automation" project → delivers an n8n workflow → moves on to the next client.

this is not a business. it is freelancing with branding.

---

finding 10: the real opportunity is not where agencies are looking

every agency targets the same buyers with the same message. "enterprise ai transformation." "custom ai agents." "ai strategy consulting."

but the actual pain we found in the data — the pain that smb owners describe in their own words — is much simpler:

- leads come in and nobody responds fast enough
- follow-up dies after the first email
- the same 50 support questions get answered manually every week
- data entry eats 10+ hours per week
- tools don't talk to each other

the $150k "ai transformation" is a solution to a problem that doesn't exist for 90% of businesses. the $5k "respond to your leads in 2 minutes instead of 2 hours" is a solution to a problem that every service business has right now.

the agencies chasing enterprise are fighting over a shrinking pool. the agencies solving specific operational pain for smbs are building recurring revenue in a market with almost no competition.

---

what this means if you're buying ai agency services

1. ask for named clients and direct references. if they can't provide them, that tells you everything.
2. ask what stack they actually use. if it's n8n + openai api, you're paying agency markup on tools you could learn in a weekend.
3. compare offshore and onshore pricing for the same deliverable. the gap is 3-5x for identical output.
4. ignore the "ai transformation" language. ask what specific process they will automate and what metric will improve.
5. avoid paying for strategy before seeing implementation. the market is full of expensive powerpoints.

what this means if you're building an ai agency

1. stop competing on "custom ai agents." the phrase means nothing and everyone uses it.
2. pick one specific pain for one specific buyer and solve it repeatedly.
3. lead with outcomes, not technology. "your leads get a response in 90 seconds" beats "we build agentic workflows."
4. price on value, not hours. a $5k project that saves $50k/year is a better business than $150/hr consulting.
5. build case studies with named clients and measurable results. in a market where nobody can prove anything, proof is the ultimate differentiator.

---

this research took weeks and covered 233 companies across 43 dimensions. the dataset is real. the numbers are verifiable.

the uncomfortable conclusion: most of the ai agency market is selling a story, not a solution. the agencies that win will be the ones that stop performing expertise and start proving results.

the rest will be features waiting to be replaced by the next platform update.

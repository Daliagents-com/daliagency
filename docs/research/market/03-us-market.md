# U.S. Market Research for Сем

<info>
Research target.
Find urgent, reachable, paid workflows in the U.S. where a Telegram-first or messaging-first autonomous agent could create measurable value for founders, AI and developer teams, marketing agencies, and small businesses.
The first commercial target remains ten customers paying roughly USD 100 each.
Date of research and source access is 2026-07-27.
</info>

<criteria>
Use only current public primary sources or high-quality authoritative sources.
Prefer official pricing, product, documentation, case studies, government statistics, and direct buyer statements.
Compare at least five credible wedges without padding weak ones into the ranking.
For every wedge, cover pain, frequency, existing spend, measurable outcome, buyer, trigger, workaround, competition, integration burden, trust risk, autonomy ceiling, reachability, and a plausible path to ten customers.
Distinguish service-backed offer, SaaS, and infrastructure.
Every material claim must sit next to a supporting URL.
</criteria>

<offer_shape>
The winning U.S. offer should start as a service-backed agent, not as a pure SaaS product.
That means one workflow, one buyer, one outcome, one human fallback, and one monthly price.
SaaS can come later, after the workflow is repeated enough to be self-serve.
Infrastructure is the connector and model layer underneath the offer, not the offer itself.
</offer_shape>

<findings>
Messaging is the default communication layer in the U.S., so the market wedge is messaging-first, not Telegram-first.
YouGov found that 85% of U.S. adults use messaging multiple times per week and that 68% say messaging has replaced at least some calls. [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54176-how-americans-communicate-in-2026-the-rise-of-messaging-ai-trends)
Pew's major-platform fact sheet does not list Telegram among the major U.S. platforms, while WhatsApp is listed, which supports the inference that Telegram should be the control surface rather than the acquisition surface in the U.S. [Pew fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)
The biggest near-term money is in immediate-response workflows where a missed lead or delayed reply costs cash the same day.
Service SMB lead rescue and agency lead follow-up satisfy that test best because the outcome is concrete, the buyer already pays for CRM or phone tooling, and the pilot can be framed around booked calls or recovered leads.
Recruiting is also strong, but it carries more compliance and data-handling weight, and Workable already sells an agentic recruiting stack.
Sales follow-up is measurable and reachable, but it is crowded by Apollo, HubSpot, and a long list of adjacent sales tools.
AI and developer team supervision is the weakest first wedge in the U.S. because the major vendors already ship remote control, mobile approvals, channels, scheduling, and agentic work surfaces.
</findings>

<top_candidates>
1. Service SMB lead rescue and booking is the best first wedge.
It has the cleanest cash-now ROI, the least abstract promise, and the easiest story to sell at roughly $100 per month.
It should be sold as a managed outcome first, not as software first.

2. Agency client ops and follow-up is the second-best wedge.
It matches David's network, rides on public HighLevel agency proof, and can be sold as a monthly operating layer above tools the buyer already pays for.
It is also the easiest place to find the first ten customers without needing a broad consumer launch.

3. Founder and RevOps sales follow-up is the third-best wedge.
It has strong existing spend and measurable outcomes, but it is more crowded and more easily absorbed by Apollo or HubSpot than the first two wedges.
If pursued, it should be a narrow managed workflow around one pipeline or one sales motion.
</top_candidates>

<disconfirming_evidence>
Telegram-first is not the U.S. market wedge.
The U.S. demand signal is messaging-first, and the public platform mix points more strongly to SMS, WhatsApp, Slack, and email than to Telegram. [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54176-how-americans-communicate-in-2026-the-rise-of-messaging-ai-trends), [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)
AI and developer teams are already covered by native vendor surfaces.
Claude Code has remote control, channels, Slack, and MCP event bridges, while OpenAI Codex has mobile approvals and live task state. [Claude Code remote control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control), [Claude Code channels](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/channels), [Claude Code MCP](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp), [OpenAI Codex from anywhere](https://openai.com/index/work-with-codex-from-anywhere/)
Sales and recruiting are also heavily covered by incumbents.
Apollo, HubSpot, and Workable already ship agentic or AI-assisted workflow layers with public pricing and public communities. [Apollo pricing](https://www.apollo.io/pricing), [HubSpot Agent Hub](https://www.hubspot.com/products/artificial-intelligence), [Workable pricing](https://www.workable.com/pricing)
That means the best wedge is not "a smarter chatbot."
It is a managed, outcome-specific workflow that sits above the tools buyers already own.
</disconfirming_evidence>

<pricing_and_unit_economics>
The first offer should probably be a setup fee plus a monthly managed fee.
For the lead-rescue wedge, a credible pilot is about $100 to $300 per month with a small setup charge when phone and CRM integration are required.
For the agency wedge, the same pilot price works if the offer is scoped to one repeatable client workflow.
For sales follow-up, a $100 monthly pilot is plausible only if it replaces a clear amount of manual rep work or recovers a measurable number of meetings.
For recruiting, pricing may need to move up once AI credits, ATS setup, and compliance review are included.
For AI and developer teams, a pure SaaS price is hard to justify because the buyer already sees low-friction native options from OpenAI and Anthropic.
The unit-economics rule is simple.
Sell the outcome, not tokens.
Use tokens and agent credits internally, but keep the buyer-facing price flat and legible.
</pricing_and_unit_economics>
